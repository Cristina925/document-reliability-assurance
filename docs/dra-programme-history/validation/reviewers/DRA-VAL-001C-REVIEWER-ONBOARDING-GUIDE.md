# DRA-VAL-001C — Reviewer Onboarding Guide

**For independent reviewers participating in the DRA Scientific Validation Programme**

---

## Welcome

You have been invited to participate as an independent reviewer in the DRA (Document Release Assurance) Scientific Validation Programme. This guide explains what you are being asked to do, why it matters, and how to do it correctly.

Please read this guide carefully before beginning any review work.

---

## 1. Scientific Purpose

DRA is an automated system designed to assess whether organisational documents meet publication-readiness standards. This programme is a scientific study to assess whether DRA's outputs align with the findings of qualified human reviewers.

The study compares evaluator outputs against your independent assessment. The goal is to determine what is true about DRA's performance — including the possibility that DRA performs poorly on some documents or document types. You are not being asked to support any particular outcome.

---

## 2. Study Boundaries

This is a scientific comparison study. It is NOT:
- A user-experience study or product assessment
- An invitation to improve DRA
- A test with right and wrong answers that you need to guess
- A study where your performance is being judged against DRA's output

Your independent professional judgement is the standard. DRA's output is compared against it — not the other way around.

---

## 3. Reviewer Independence

Your independence is the most important property you bring to this study. To preserve it:
- You must not view DRA evaluator outputs before submitting your review.
- You must not discuss your findings with other reviewers before submitting.
- You must not seek guidance that might reveal what DRA found.
- You must not coordinate submissions with other programme participants.

Any violation of independence requirements must be reported and may result in withdrawal of your submission.

---

## 4. Required Materials

Before beginning review work you must have:
- [ ] Completed the confidentiality and consent agreement
- [ ] Completed and received a cleared conflict-of-interest assessment
- [ ] Completed all required qualification exercises
- [ ] Received your assignment pack from the programme
- [ ] Confirmed your availability for the review window

Do not begin reviewing documents until all requirements are confirmed.

---

## 5. Review Sequence

For each assigned document:

1. **Read the document** — read the entire document before recording any findings.
2. **Identify issues** — identify all release-significant issues using your professional judgement and the issue taxonomy in the review protocol.
3. **Record each issue separately** — each distinct issue gets its own record. Do not combine separate issues.
4. **Assess materiality** — classify each issue as material or not material.
5. **Assess severity** — classify each issue as CRITICAL, SIGNIFICANT, or ADVISORY using the definitions in the review protocol.
6. **Compare against source evidence** — where source documents are provided, compare the document's claims against them and record any discrepancies as evidence-gap issues.
7. **Record uncertainty** — where you are uncertain about an issue's existence, materiality, or severity, record your uncertainty explicitly. Do not omit issues you are uncertain about.
8. **Form a release recommendation** — at the end of your review, record whether you recommend SUPPORTED, REVIEW, or HOLD, with your rationale.
9. **Submit** — submit your findings in the format specified. Do not modify your submission after freezing.

---

## 6. Source-Evidence Use

Where source documents are provided alongside a corpus document, you must use them. The purpose is to compare the document's claims against the source material.

Source evidence use is mandatory for evidence-gap issue identification. If source material contains discrepancies with the document, record each discrepancy as a separate issue.

---

## 7. Issue Recording

Record every issue you identify, including:
- Issues where you are uncertain of severity
- Issues where you are uncertain of materiality
- Issues that are present but trivial (record as ADVISORY)
- Issues that the document author may have been unaware of

Do not filter out issues on the basis that DRA might not find them. Record your complete professional assessment.

---

## 8. Uncertainty

Uncertainty is important data. When you are uncertain:
- Record the issue anyway
- Mark your confidence level as LOW or UNCERTAIN
- Explain the source of your uncertainty in the issue rationale

Overclaiming confidence where it does not exist distorts the comparison.

---

## 9. Severity

Severity classifications:
- **CRITICAL** — a defect that, in your professional judgement, must be resolved before the document is published or relied upon
- **SIGNIFICANT** — a material defect that should be addressed before publication; may affect reliance on key sections
- **ADVISORY** — a minor issue that does not block publication but should be noted for improvement

Use the definitions precisely and consistently across all assigned documents.

---

## 10. Release Recommendation

At the end of each document review, record one of:
- **SUPPORTED** — you are satisfied the document meets publication-readiness standards, based on your review
- **REVIEW** — you have identified issues that require consideration; document may be acceptable with caveats
- **HOLD** — you have identified CRITICAL issues that must be resolved before the document can be released

Your recommendation must be consistent with your severity assessments.

---

## 11. Prohibited Coordination

Before submission is frozen, you must not:
- Discuss your findings with other reviewers assigned to the same document
- Review another reviewer's submitted findings
- Review evaluator outputs for any document you are reviewing
- Seek advice from colleagues that might reveal expected findings

After submission is frozen, de-briefing discussions are permitted and encouraged.

---

## 12. Evaluator Blinding

You will not be told what DRA found on any document until after your independent submission has been received. This is not an oversight — it is a scientific requirement. If you accidentally encounter evaluator outputs before submitting, report this immediately. Your submission may need to be excluded.

---

## 13. Document Security

- Store assigned documents securely. Do not save them to public cloud storage, shared drives, or any location accessible to others without programme authorisation.
- Do not upload documents into external AI systems.
- Delete documents from your devices when the programme confirms your submission is complete.

---

## 14. Time Recording

Record the time you spend on each document. This data helps the programme assess reviewer workload and refine protocol efficiency. Time recording is separate from your review submissions and does not affect your findings.

---

## 15. Submission Freeze

Once you freeze a submission, it cannot be changed. The programme records the freeze timestamp. If you believe you have made a material error before freezing, contact the programme team — do not freeze and then request changes.

---

## 16. Correction Policy

Post-freeze corrections are not permitted unless the programme determines that a material procedural error occurred (for example, you reviewed the wrong document). Correction requests must be submitted in writing within 24 hours of discovery and are subject to programme approval.

---

## 17. Withdrawal

You may withdraw at any time before your submission is frozen. Contact the programme team and your slot will be reassigned. No penalty or negative consequence results from withdrawal.

After freeze, withdrawal from the aggregate analysis is subject to the consent agreement limitations.

---

## 18. Escalation

Report the following immediately to the programme team:
- Accidental access to evaluator outputs before submission
- Any possible breach of confidentiality
- Technical problems with document access
- Any concern that your independence may have been compromised
- Any new conflict of interest arising after qualification

Contact details are provided in your assignment pack.

---

## A Note on DRA's Status

DRA is not described in this programme as proven, validated, superior, or commercially ready. The purpose of this programme is to gather scientific evidence. You are helping produce that evidence. Whatever the results show, they will be reported honestly and completely.

Thank you for your contribution to independent scientific validation.
