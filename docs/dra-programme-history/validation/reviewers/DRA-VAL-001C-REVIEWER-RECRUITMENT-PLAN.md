# DRA-VAL-001C — Reviewer Recruitment Plan

**Status: OPERATIVE** | Effective from DRA-VAL-001C milestone activation (2026-07-27)

---

## 1. Recruitment Objectives

The DRA Scientific Validation Programme requires a pool of genuine, independent, qualified human reviewers to produce the adjudicated human reference standard against which DRA evaluator outputs will be compared.

The reviewer pool must be:
- **Independent** — no financial, employment, academic, or personal relationship with Runtime Governance Labs, CTS, DRA, or the evaluator development process.
- **Qualified** — demonstrated professional competence in document review, evidence assessment, and/or relevant domain expertise.
- **Diverse** — covering all nine corpus domains with sufficient redundancy to handle withdrawals.
- **Honest** — not recruited to produce a favourable result; recruited to produce an accurate one.

Recruitment must not proceed by fabricating reviewers. Where genuine reviewers are not available, the programme records the gap and the steps required to fill it.

---

## 2. Reviewer Categories

### 2.1 General Assurance Reviewer

Professionals with demonstrated experience reviewing consequential documents, assurance outputs, compliance materials, risk reports, governance documents, or equivalent. Assigned to documents within their competence. Do not need domain specialist depth but must demonstrate general critical analysis capability.

### 2.2 Domain Specialist

Professionals with domain-specific expertise in at least one of the nine corpus domains. Domain specialists are assigned preferentially to documents in their area. Domain expertise must be evidence-backed.

### 2.3 Independent Adjudicator

Qualified reviewers who additionally have seniority or evidence-evaluation experience sufficient to resolve material disagreements between primary reviewer submissions. Adjudicators must first be qualified as general reviewers or domain specialists. An adjudicator may not adjudicate a document they reviewed as a primary reviewer.

---

## 3. Target Pool Sizes

The required number of reviewers depends on expertise overlap, workload limits, declared conflicts, and availability. Headcount alone is insufficient.

### 3.1 Pilot Minimum (DRA-VAL-001D gate)

- **6 qualified reviewers** (general and/or specialist)
- At least **2 general assurance reviewers**
- At least **3 domain specialists** (covering represented pilot domains)
- At least **1 independent adjudicator**
- Sufficient coverage to allocate **2 independent reviewers per frozen pilot document**

With 7 pilot documents and 2 reviewers per document, a minimum of **14 reviewer-document assignments** are required. With a maximum workload of 5 documents per reviewer, this requires at least **3 reviewers**, but workload limits, availability, and conflicts will raise the practical minimum.

### 3.2 Preferred Pilot Pool

- **10–12 qualified reviewers**
- At least 2 reviewers capable of covering each represented pilot domain
- At least **2 independent adjudicators**
- No reviewer assigned beyond the workload limit defined in §10

### 3.3 Full 120-Document Benchmark Planning Target

- **18–30 qualified reviewers**
- Domain coverage sufficient for all 9 corpus domains
- An adjudicator pool independent from initial review assignments where feasible

## 5. General Assurance Reviewer Requirements

General assurance reviewers must demonstrate:
- Professional experience reviewing consequential documents, evidence, controls, assurance outputs, compliance materials, risk reports, technical reports, policies, or equivalent
- Ability to distinguish unsupported claims, evidence gaps, authority gaps, scope problems, contradictions, and release-significant defects
- Ability to document findings clearly and consistently
- Ability to follow a frozen review protocol without deviation
- No disqualifying conflict of interest
- Completion of general qualification exercises

See `DRA-VAL-001C-REVIEWER-ELIGIBILITY-STANDARD.md` for full requirements.

---

## 6. Adjudicator Requirements

Adjudicators must:
- First qualify as a general assurance reviewer or domain specialist
- Demonstrate seniority or evidence-evaluation experience beyond general review competence
- Demonstrate ability to resolve issue-matching and decision disagreements
- Not adjudicate documents they reviewed as primary reviewer
- Accept the frozen comparison and adjudication rules

## 7. Recruitment Channels

The following channels are available for genuine recruitment. All outreach must be documented in the Recruitment Outreach Tracker (`DRA-VAL-001C-RECRUITMENT-OUTREACH-TRACKER.md`).

| Channel | Likely Category |
|---------|-----------------|
| Professional networks (LinkedIn, professional associations) | General and specialist |
| Academic contacts (university researchers, PhD supervisors) | Specialist |
| Regulatory community contacts | Legal, healthcare, finance, cybersecurity |
| Internal referrals from trusted contacts (with COI check) | General |
| Open call via professional bodies | General and specialist |
| Research programme participant registers | General |

Recruitment through any channel that introduces a conflict of interest is prohibited.

---

## 8. Outreach Sequence

1. **Identify prospect** — record in Outreach Tracker with domain, source, and conflict-risk assessment.
2. **Initial contact** — send programme overview and eligibility summary. Do not share scientific corpus documents.
3. **Expression of interest** — prospect confirms interest and completes initial conflict-risk disclosure.
4. **Application invitation** — invite eligible prospects to complete the formal application.
5. **Application** — formal application submitted.
6. **Screening** — programme screens application for eligibility, evidence sufficiency, and conflict risk.
7. **Qualification exercises** — eligible applicants invited to complete exercises.
8. **Onboarding** — qualified reviewers complete consent and confidentiality processes.
9. **Assignment** — qualified, consented reviewers added to the eligible pool.

---

## 9. Application Process

Applications are submitted via the structured form defined in `DRA-VAL-001C-REVIEWER-APPLICATION.md`. Applications must include:
- Genuine contact details
- Professional experience and domain expertise evidence
- Preliminary conflict-of-interest disclosure
- Availability declaration

Applications without supporting evidence are rejected at screening.

---

## 10. Workload Limits

| Reviewer Type | Max Pilot Documents | Max Full-Benchmark Documents | Max Adjudication Documents |
|--------------|--------------------|-----------------------------|---------------------------|
| Default | 5 | 20 | 5 |
| Domain specialist (deep expertise, limited availability) | 3 | 15 | 3 |
| Preferred pilot reviewer | 7 | 25 | 7 |
| Adjudicator-only | 0 (primary) | 0 (primary) | 10 |

Individual workload limits may be set below these defaults based on stated availability or conflict restrictions.

---

## 11. Confidentiality Process

Reviewers must complete the confidentiality agreement defined in `DRA-VAL-001C-REVIEWER-CONFIDENTIALITY-AND-CONSENT.md` before accessing any corpus document. The agreement covers:
- Non-disclosure of corpus document content
- Prohibition on sharing benchmark materials
- Prohibition on uploading materials to unauthorised AI systems
- Prohibition on seeking evaluator output
- Non-coordination before submission

---

## 12. Conflict-of-Interest Process

Every applicant must submit a complete COI declaration using the form in `DRA-VAL-001C-CONFLICT-OF-INTEREST-DECLARATION.md`. Conflicts are assessed independently — the reviewer's own assessment is not binding. Disqualifying conflicts block participation. Manageable conflicts are documented with restrictions.

---

## 13. Consent Process

Reviewers must give explicit informed consent using the consent framework in `DRA-VAL-001C-REVIEWER-CONFIDENTIALITY-AND-CONSENT.md`. Consent is never assumed or implied. Consent status is tracked per reviewer.

---

## 14. Compensation Policy

Compensation arrangements (where applicable) are agreed individually before assignment. The programme does not offer results-linked compensation. Compensation is disclosed in the COI declaration if it creates a relevant interest.

---

## 15. Withdrawal Process

Reviewers may withdraw at any time before submission. Withdrawal is recorded in the registry. Withdrawn reviewers are not assigned to further documents. Withdrawal after submission freeze is subject to the consent withdrawal terms.

---

## 16. Replacement Process

If a reviewer withdraws or is suspended mid-assignment, a replacement is sourced from the eligible pool. If no replacement is available, the document slot is flagged as under-assigned. The scientific lead assesses the impact on the comparison protocol.

---

## 17. Availability Management

Reviewer availability is confirmed at application, re-confirmed at qualification, and re-confirmed before assignment. Reviewers who do not confirm availability within the review window are treated as unavailable and excluded from assignment calculations.

---

## 18. Reviewer-Retention Policy

The programme aims to retain qualified reviewers across the pilot and full benchmark. Retention measures include timely communication, clear scheduling, and respectful feedback. Reviewers are not pressured to produce particular findings.

---

## 19. Failed Recruitment Handling

If the minimum pilot reviewer pool cannot be genuinely assembled:
1. The programme records the specific unmet requirements in the Reviewer Registry.
2. The Pilot Reviewer Readiness Assessment records `NOT_READY`.
3. DRA-VAL-001D does not proceed until the gap is resolved.
4. The programme identifies the specific external recruitment actions needed.

No fabricated, simulated, or placeholder reviewers are admitted as a substitute for genuine recruitment.

---

## 20. Incomplete-Domain Handling

If a pilot domain has fewer than the required reviewers due to recruitment failure:
1. The domain is recorded as `INSUFFICIENT` or `NO_COVERAGE` in the coverage matrix.
2. Documents in that domain cannot be assigned until coverage is achieved.
3. The programme may defer those documents to a later execution window.
4. The pilot corpus partial execution is documented as a protocol deviation.
